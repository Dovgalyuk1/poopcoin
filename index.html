01 — the structure

THREE FLOORS

floor 3

THE CURL

the pointy bit on top that catches all the light in every photo ever taken of me. it does not carry anything, it does not hold anything up, it simply sits there being polished by the two floors underneath it. every single person who arrives here is completely certain they will end up as the curl.

floor 2

THE MIDDLE

wider, heavier, slightly less shiny, and permanently looking upwards. the middle floor spends its entire life explaining to the base floor how close it is to becoming the curl. it has been saying this since the day i was stacked and it has never once been wrong on purpose.

floor 1

THE BASE

the widest one, the one doing all the actual work, the one you are standing on right now whether you like the news or not. if the base ever decides to step out for five minutes the other two floors become a puddle with excellent branding.

how you move up

you bring three people. those three people each bring three people. those nine people each bring three people. one floor gets added underneath you, which technically means you went up, and the curl stays exactly where it has always been, which technically means nothing happened.

the stairs are on the inside. they are real stairs, they are well lit, they are swept every morning. they only go around.

03 — what is this

WHAT IS THIS

the rule is older than money and every grandmother on earth knows it: you step in something like me, you say a word you would not say in front of your mother, and then money arrives. nobody has ever explained the mechanism. i stopped asking somewhere around the year 1400 and started charging instead.

this is the whole coin, and it fits in one line: step in it, money follows. i am the shiniest thing you will ever have the good fortune to ruin your shoes on, and i am standing right here in the open, chrome-plated, patient, completely unbothered by the smell of my own success.

people show up every day with clean shoes and a very simple plan: touch it once, get lucky, leave immediately. nobody leaves immediately. the shine gets into your head, and then you are on the internet at three in the morning explaining to a stranger why a pile of money-luck deserves a chart.

step in me as many times as you like. it is free, it works, and the only real cost is that from now on everyone at your table knows exactly what you have been doing with your evening. wipe your shoes on the way out, or do not. the money does not check.
